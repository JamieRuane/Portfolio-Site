About me

I'm Jamie, a student at the University of Limerick who likes building things people actually use, from neon arcade racers to courses that teach nine programming languages.

I care about the details that make software feel good: procedurally generated audio in a racing game, exercises that are checked against a real compiler, a CLI with zero dependencies. Most of what I build starts as something I wanted to exist, and I keep going until it's polished enough to share.

Learn more

Who I am professionally, my strengths and what I value.

I'm a first-year Immersive Software Engineering student at the University of Limerick, and I'm happiest when I'm building something real from start to finish.

I like to work end to end: designing an idea, writing the code, testing it properly and shipping it so other people can use it. I'm drawn to backend, cloud and DevOps work, and to using AI to automate the repetitive parts of people's jobs. What drives me is seeing something I built make a difference, whether that's a student learning to code on Code Quest or a developer saving ten minutes on their stand-up. I want to grow into an engineer who can lead technical teams and, one day, build products and companies of my own.

Key strengths

  • 01

    Shipping complete projects

    I finish what I start. Code Quest has over 300 lessons across nine languages, standup-scribe is published on npm, and my AWS platform runs with automated CI/CD and monitoring.

  • 02

    Fast, self-directed learning

    I teach myself what a project needs, from AWS CDK and Docker to Three.js, and I'm building a mini C compiler purely to understand how C and assembly really work.

  • 03

    Leadership and representation

    I was elected by my peers as Chairperson of my school's student council in consecutive years, chairing meetings and representing students' views to staff.

  • 04

    Consistency under pressure

    I achieved 625 points with an H1 in all seven Leaving Certificate subjects, and I now balance full-time study with a part-time job and my own projects.

  • 05

    Communication

    From representing a whole student body to fundraising and speaking about the Hope Foundation's work, I'm comfortable explaining ideas clearly to different audiences.

Three values that guide how I work

These come from UL's Cultural Charter, which I explored in the ISE values workshop. They're the three that best describe how I already try to work, and who I want to be as an engineer.

value 01

Ambitious and driven

Work with others to push the boundaries of what's possible, and stick with the hard problem.

Why it matters to me
My Leaving Cert taught me what sustained effort can achieve. Aiming high gives me a reason to keep improving, and I'd rather try something ambitious and learn from it than settle for something easy.
A time I showed it
Code Quest started as one small course and grew into 300+ interactive lessons across nine programming languages, each checked against a real compiler. I kept going long after it would have been easy to stop.
How it shapes how I work with others
I try to set the standard by showing up prepared and doing my share. I'd rather lift a team's ambition by example than push my own pace onto other people.
How it fits my career goals
ISE aims to create future CEOs and CTOs. Ambition is what will push me to take on stretch residencies, lead technical work, and build Ampel, the AI automation business I'm starting for Irish SMEs.
How I'll show it this year
Apply for a residency that stretches me, and deliver Ampel's first real client project.
value 02

Creative and innovative

Look at things differently, weigh the risks, then trust my judgement.

Why it matters to me
Most of what I build starts as something I wished existed. Creativity is how I turn frustrations into tools, and it's what makes software engineering fun for me.
A time I showed it
For Drift City I had no art or audio assets, so I generated every 3D model, texture, sound effect and music track in code. A limitation became the game's defining feature.
How it shapes how I work with others
I'll suggest the bold idea and ask "why?" in code reviews, but I also make room for other people's ideas. The best solution often comes from combining them.
How it fits my career goals
I want to work on products, not just tickets. Creative problem solving is what lets an engineer spot opportunities like standup-scribe or Taster, and it's essential for building a startup.
How I'll show it this year
Pitch an unconventional solution at a hackathon or during residency, and keep shipping my own ideas.
value 03

Ethical and sustainable

Do the right thing, in the right way, and take on societal challenges.

Why it matters to me
Travelling to Kolkata with the Hope Foundation showed me how much difference doing the right thing can make to real people. Software affects people too, so how we build it matters as much as what we build.
A time I showed it
I raised over €2,500 for the Hope Foundation. In my own engineering, I gate releases on tests and code review, and I'm open about where I use AI tools, so I never ship code I couldn't explain.
How it shapes how I work with others
I'm honest early: I raise a problem on Tuesday rather than hide it until Friday, I own my bugs, and I give credit where it's due.
How it fits my career goals
I want to build technology people can trust. That means responsible use of AI in the automations I build, and thinking about the environmental cost of software, a lesson from Birgit Penzenstadler's talk.
How I'll show it this year
Label AI use honestly in my project write-ups, and consider sustainability when designing my own systems.

Hackathons & networking

hackathon

Reddit Daily Game Hackathon

I'm building middLE, a daily game that runs inside Reddit posts, using Reddit's Devvit developer platform.

What I did
Designed the game, built the client and server, and playtested it daily in a test subreddit, shipping fixes through pull requests as problems showed up.
What I learned
Working within a real platform's rules, limits and deadline. Players behave in ways you don't plan for, so watching them play matters more than the first design.
What I'll do next
Submit by the 9 December deadline, then share middLE and the write-up on LinkedIn.

volunteering · fundraising

Hope Foundation ambassador

I raised over €2,500 for the Hope Foundation, then travelled to Kolkata, India in March 2024 as one of its ambassadors to see the work the money supports.

The money went directly to the foundation's work with children and families in Kolkata. Telling people at home about it afterwards also helped build awareness and support beyond the trip itself.

Seeing the work first-hand put my own circumstances in perspective. It showed me how much difference organised, sustained effort can make, and made me want my own work to have a real effect on people.

Where am I going next?

My development plan for the next 12 months.

At the start of the module I thought of myself as someone who codes. I now think of myself as an emerging engineer: someone who is judged on how they work with a team, how reliable their software is, and how they treat the people it affects. I want employers to see me as reliable, ambitious and easy to work with. What still needs work is professional experience: I haven't yet worked in an engineering team inside a company, and my LinkedIn and network are only starting out.

Four goals

  1. target · April 2027

    Secure a backend, cloud or DevOps residency

    Why it matters
    Residency is where I get the team experience my profile is missing, in the area I want to build a career in.
    Actions
    Research every residency partner's tech stack, tailor my CV for each application, and practise technical interviews weekly.
  2. target · June 2027

    Pass the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner exam

    Why it matters
    It turns self-taught AWS experience into a recognised, measurable credential employers can trust.
    Actions
    Complete the AWS Skill Builder learning path, take two practice exams scoring 80%+, then book the exam.
  3. target · September 2027

    Deliver Ampel's first paid client project

    Why it matters
    Building a business is my long-term goal. One real client tests whether I can turn technical skill into value for someone else.
    Actions
    Interview ten local SMEs about their admin pain points, build one automation demo, and deliver a scoped first project.
  4. target · November 2027

    Build a professional network

    Why it matters
    Every talk this year showed me that opportunities come through people, and my network is only starting.
    Actions
    Complete my LinkedIn profile, connect with 50 people in the industry, and attend at least two hackathons or meetups.
